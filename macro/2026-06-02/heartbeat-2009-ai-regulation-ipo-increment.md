## Incremental facts
1. **U.S. AI regulation uncertainty / possible pre-release model review**
   - WIRED reports internal uncertainty in the U.S. administration over reviving an AI executive-order framework after a planned signing was canceled on May 21.
   - The most contentious reported provision was a voluntary framework for frontier labs such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google to provide early model access to the White House before public release for cybersecurity evaluation; the draft reportedly contemplated submission up to 90 days pre-release.
   - Macro relevance: AI policy is being framed more explicitly as national-security / cyber-capability infrastructure, not only consumer protection or platform governance.

2. **OpenAI policy / political advocacy statement**
   - Official OpenAI page says AI governance should involve governments, researchers, workers, civil society, independent experts and the public; OpenAI says it has not donated to super PACs, candidates or campaigns and does not have an employee-funded PAC.
   - Macro relevance: confirms large AI labs are moving into explicit policy-positioning and political-economy risk management as regulation heats up.

3. **Anthropic confidential draft S-1**
   - Official Anthropic post says it confidentially submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1 to the SEC for a proposed IPO; share count and price are not set, and timing depends on SEC review and market conditions.
   - Macro relevance: frontier AI labs are moving toward public-market absorption; this matters for AI capital-cycle valuation, index/public-equity liquidity, and hyperscaler/AI-infra capex sentiment.
